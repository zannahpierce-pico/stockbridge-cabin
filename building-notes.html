5. Build the railing

Heights off the deck: bottom rail 2" to 5 1/2", balusters, top rail 31 1/2" to 35", cap 35" to 36 1/2".

Step
Re-measure the two uncertain spans (above), and check every post is plumb.
Trim every post to 35" off the deck, so the cap sits on all of them.
Cut each section's top and bottom rails together, the same length.
Clamp each pair of rails side by side and mark the balusters on both at once: 4 1/2" on centre, centred in the section, so the gap at each end is the same and under 4". Counts per section are in the table below.
Screw a baluster connector on each mark (top face of the bottom rail, underside of the top rail) with the stainless #8 screws, 2 each, not the screws in the packs. Run a thin bead of the black sealant round each connector if using it.
Dry-fit one baluster between a pair of rails to check the 26" works out with the bottom rail 2" off the deck, before doing the rest.
Assemble each section like a ladder on the sawhorses, then set it between its posts: the top rail flush with the post tops (35"), the bottom rail on 2" blocks.
Fix each rail end to its post with 3" screws angled in, 2 per end, with 1/8" pilot holes so the ends don't split. Check level.
Cut the cap pieces (cut list above), dry-fitting each corner before cutting the next piece.
Screw the cap down: 2 screws into every post (both sides of a joint), and one into the top rail about every foot.
Push hard on the top of each post. If one flexes, add a Simpson DTT2Z tension tie at its base (a guard should hold 200 lb pushed sideways).

6. Build the gates

Each gate slides along the deck side of the railing and parks behind the rail beside its stairs: the back one toward the porch side (so it never crosses the red door's swing), the front one toward the bank side. It rides on two casters, and a pipe along its top and bottom slides through eyes on the stair post. The latch post has no eyes.

Step
Re-measure each opening. Gate length = the opening + 5 1/2" (back: 48 1/4 → 53 3/4; front: 50 → 55 1/2), so it covers the opening and the stair post and ends about 2" past it.
Paint first, so it dries: wipe and scuff the pipes, then spray the pipes, caps, eyes and caster plates flat black.
Frame: the stiles (24 5/16") go between the top and bottom rails. Two 3" screws through the rail into each stile end, with pilot holes. Check it's square (equal diagonals), then screw an L bracket into each inside corner with the stainless #8 screws.
Balusters: 11 per gate, cut to fit between the rails (about 24 5/16") with a hacksaw; file the cut end, and the connector goes on it. Space them like the railing: 4 1/2" on centre, centred, end gaps under 4".
Casters: screw one under the bottom rail about 4" in from each end, the wheels in line with the gate. Their plate is as wide as the 2x4, so no blocks. 5/32" pilots for the 1/4" lag screws (or use the screws in the box). The gate then sits about 2 3/4" off the deck.
Gate eyes, 4 per gate: on the face toward the railing, 1" in from each end, centred on the top rail and on the bottom rail. 1/4" pilot; turn them in with a screwdriver through the eye, the holes facing along the gate. If a tip comes through the back, file it flush.
Stand the gate in place on its casters, about 2 3/4" off the stair post's face. Run a string through the gate's top eyes past the stair post, and put the post's top eye exactly on that line; the same for the bottom. The eyes are a snug fit on the pipe, so if they're off line the pipe binds.
Pipes: with one cap on, slide the pipe through the gate's trailing eye, the stair post's eye and the gate's leading eye, then screw on the other cap. The caps catch on the eyes (no washers needed). The few spare inches stick out past the leading end.
Latch: the catch on the latch post, on the side facing the opening; the bar on the gate's leading stile, pointing back toward the rail, so closing the gate rides the bar up the catch's ramp and it drops in. Test it a few times.
Add a stop (a block on the rail or the deck) so the open gate's leading eye can't run into the stair post's eye.
Slide each gate open and closed. If it binds, move the stair post's eyes until the pipe runs free.

Gate details:

  • The front gate parks along the 44 1/2" and 44 5/8" sections (about 89" of rail before the corner roof post); it can't go toward the driveway side, where the porch-roof post stands just inside the rail. The back gate parks along the 36" and 38 5/8" sections.
  • No eyes on the latch post: a capped pipe end would have to go into it each time the gate closes, and it won't. No eyes on the posts in between either: the gate's own end eyes would run into them. The stair post's eyes and the casters keep the gate upright, and the latch holds the closed end.
  • Casters: Luomorgo 2" fixed, black rubber, about 2.7" tall, plate 2.3" x 1.5" with 6.5 mm holes, 66 lb each (from a reseller's listing: measure one).
  • Eyes: 1/2" galvanized pipe (0.84" across) through 3/8" x 3 7/8" zinc eyes, tested 2026-10-29: snug, and the caps won't pass through. Each eye stands roughly 1 3/8" off the wood (not measured).
  • Where the paint wears off the pipes from sliding, the galvanizing underneath still won't rust.

Choices made along the way:

  • The video used 3/4" pipe with 5" eyes (1 1/4" inside) and 3/4" caps. We used 1/2" pipe (about $50 less, and the 3/4" would have come too late).
  • Set aside: the ZAUGONTW 5" eyes from Amazon (cancelled once the Lowe's eyes fitted), the Everbilt "1 in." eye at Home Depot (0.63" inside, too small), 7/8" washers under the caps (not needed with the snug eyes), POWERTEC 3" casters on blocks (the 2" ones screw straight on and leave a smaller gap under the gate for the dogs), 2x4x12 boards for the gates (the 10's and 8's cover them).
  • The screws in the baluster packs are black M5 x 30 flat-heads, like drywall screws, and would rust in treated wood: hence the stainless #8s. The connector is a cup the screw head sits in, and the baluster hides it.
  • The connectors in the packs are for level rails only; the stairs will need angled ones (below).

Notes on fit

  • Couch rug: 8' is 5" wider than the floor under the loft, so it runs that far past the loft edge toward the kitchen. A 7'-6" square would stay inside the loft edge. The front edge of an 8' rug ends at the foot of the ladder and about 3'-6" from the centre of the wood stove; check the stove's label for floor protection.
  • Couch rug and kitchen rug: the kitchen rug ends about 1" before the couch rug begins, and their corners line up for about 10".
  • Kitchen rug: leaves about 4" of floor each side in the aisle. It stops about 1'-8" short of the end of the counter and 2'-8" short of the first stool.
  • Loft rug: 7'-6" leaves about 1" in a 7'-7" loft, and the railing posts take about 1 1/2" of that at floor level. The rug has to slide under the railing's bottom rail and may need to curl slightly at the posts. Measure wall to railing post before ordering; rugs also run an inch or two off their stated size. If it is too tight, 6' x 9' is the next size down.
  • Loft rug: keep the front edge at least 1' back from where the ladder steps off, and use a rug pad.

Martin MDV8P: looked into further (2026-10-07)

Easy to buy in the USA: several off-grid and cabin suppliers list it in stock, at about $640 to $930 (an earlier note here that it was discontinued was wrong; that came from one Canadian distributor's page). Details below are from the owner's manual, which covers the MDV8, MDV12 and MDV20.

  • Heat: 8,000 BTU in, 6,400 out, turning down to 2,900. Sellers rate it for about 194 sq ft. The MDV12 is the next size up: 11,000 in, 8,800 out.
  • No electricity. Standing pilot lit with a push-button igniter, a HI to LO thermostat knob, and a safety that shuts the gas if the pilot goes out. So it can be left on low as a freeze guard.
  • Size: 22 5/8" high x 11 13/16" wide x 7 1/8" deep, about 23 lb.
  • Vent: straight out through the wall directly behind the heater, sloping slightly down to the outside. The vent kit is in the box. The hood sticks out 4 5/16" outside.
  • Wall thickness: must be between 4 1/2" and 12 5/8". CHECK the cabin's walls: the model has them at about 4 1/2", right at the minimum.
  • Clearances to things that burn: the bottom of the heater at least 7" off the floor, 6" each side, 24" above, about 3/4" behind (it stands off the wall on spacers).
  • Vent outside: at least 12" above the ground or a deck, 6" from a window or door that opens, 24" from an outside wall that meets this one at a corner. Under a porch or deck roof it needs local approval.
  • Warranty: one year on parts, lifetime on the combustion chamber, for the original owner; it says installation must be by a licensed technician.
  • Sold in North America by Bismar Inc. of Boucherville, Quebec. Who manufactures it is not stated.
  • Reviews found were for the 20,000 BTU model and were mixed: easy to install and decent heat, but complaints about the thermostat's accuracy and noise.

Where the Martin would fit in this cabin. It needs a patch of outside wall about 24" wide that is clear from 7" to about 54" above the floor (7" + 22 5/8" + 24").
