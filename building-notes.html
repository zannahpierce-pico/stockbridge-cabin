Notes on fit

  • Couch rug: 8' is 5" wider than the floor under the loft, so it runs that far past the loft edge toward the kitchen. A 7'-6" square would stay inside the loft edge. The front edge of an 8' rug ends at the foot of the ladder and about 3'-6" from the centre of the wood stove; check the stove's label for floor protection.
  • Couch rug and kitchen rug: the kitchen rug ends about 1" before the couch rug begins, and their corners line up for about 10".
  • Kitchen rug: leaves about 4" of floor each side in the aisle. It stops about 1'-8" short of the end of the counter and 2'-8" short of the first stool.
  • Loft rug: 7'-6" leaves about 1" in a 7'-7" loft, and the railing posts take about 1 1/2" of that at floor level. The rug has to slide under the railing's bottom rail and may need to curl slightly at the posts. Measure wall to railing post before ordering; rugs also run an inch or two off their stated size. If it is too tight, 6' x 9' is the next size down.
  • Loft rug: keep the front edge at least 1' back from where the ladder steps off, and use a rug pad.

Wiring research and plan

Researched 2026-10-06. These are working notes from web sources and general knowledge of the National Electrical Code (NEC), not a substitute for the code text, the equipment manuals or an electrician. Items marked CHECK are not confirmed.

Who can do the work (Vermont)

  • 26 V.S.A. § 910: an owner may do electrical work in their own freestanding single-unit residence and its outbuildings without a licence.
  • The Vermont Electrical Safety Rules exempt a single-family owner-occupied dwelling from the state work-notice and inspection process (the exemption does not cover rentals). The work is still supposed to meet the adopted NEC.
  • Vermont's rules were reissued in 2023 and 2025. CHECK which NEC edition the 2025 rules adopt (2020 or 2023).

The power source and "panel" (confirmed by the owner 2026-10-06)

The owners already have both the F2000 power station and the transfer switch (2026-10-07).

  • Anker SOLIX F2000: 120 V only, 2,400 W continuous (20 A). No 240 V. The cabin will never have utility power and probably no solar; at most a bigger battery later (the F3000 is also 120 V, 30 A, and uses the same L5-30 inlet).
  • The "panel" is the Anker SOLIX 120V Transfer Switch, SKU B17B71A1: a Reliance Pro/Tran 2, 30 A, six 15 A single-pole circuits, L5-30 inlet on the unit, wattmeters, metal cabinet for indoor surface mounting. About 7 3/4" x 11 1/4" x 4 1/2" deep (size taken from sister models; CHECK the manual).
  • Owner's plan: the switch's wire whip ends in its own small junction box, and the breakers are jumpered: there is no main panel. Read as: in the junction box each circuit's LOAD wire (red) is spliced to one branch circuit; the LINE wires (black, meant for a utility breaker) are capped off individually; the whip's white is the common neutral for all six circuits; green is the ground. The switches stay on GEN. The unit's own six 15 A breakers protect the branch circuits. CHECK this reading with whoever wires it.
  • Consequences:
    • Everything starts at that junction box: six circuits, 15 A each, 14 AWG. Total limited by the battery (20 A now).
    • The induction burner (about 1,800 W, 15 A) needs a circuit to itself.
    • Junction box: six branch cables plus the whip's 14 conductors is a lot of fill. Use at least 6" x 6" x 4"; drawn as 8" x 8" x 4".
    • One shared neutral in the whip serves all six circuits. That is how the unit is built (it is sized for the 30 A inlet); do not add any other shared-neutral arrangement downstream.
    • No AFCI protection is possible from these breakers. GFCI protection comes from GFCI receptacles at the first outlet of each circuit that needs it (kitchen, bath, outdoor).
    • CHECK grounding: whether the F2000 bonds neutral to ground internally. Neutral and ground must be bonded in exactly one place. With no service there is also no grounding electrode; ask the installer whether one is wanted for the outdoor circuits.
    • The unit stands 4 1/2" off the wall, so it cannot hide flat behind a painting. Modelled with the painting as the hinged door of a shallow cabinet over it, open at the bottom for the battery cord.

Routing cable through framing

  • NM-B ("Romex") through bored holes in wood: keep the edge of the hole at least 1-1/4" from the nearest edge of the stud, or cover it with a 1/16" steel nail plate (NEC 300.4(A)). In a 2x4 (3-1/2") a centred hole up to 1" just meets this.
  • Support: secure within 12" of each box (8" for a single-gang plastic box without a clamp) and at least every 4'-6" (NEC 334.30). Passing through bored holes counts as support. Staple flat cable flat, not on edge.
  • Studs (IRC R602.6): holes up to 40% of stud width in bearing and exterior walls (60% in non-bearing, or if the stud is doubled); hole edge at least 5/8" from the stud edge; notches up to 25% (bearing) or 40% (non-bearing).
  • Joists and rafters (IRC R502.8 / R802.7): holes no bigger than 1/3 of the depth, at least 2" from the top and bottom edges and from other holes; notches no deeper than 1/6 of the depth, never in the middle third of the span.
  • Do not drill or notch headers (the owners have already ruled out the header between the windows and the transoms).

Cabin-specific routing problems

  • Loft floor = ceiling below. The loft boards sit directly on exposed timber joists: there is no cavity. Under-loft lights, the bath light box and anything fixed to the loft boards need either cable run on top of the joists in a routed channel under the loft flooring, or surface raceway / conduit along the joists. NM cable cannot just be stapled exposed where it can be damaged.
  • Roof. The rafter bays will be insulated and then covered. All cable for the high-ceiling lights must be in before insulation. Run along the side of a rafter or through holes within the limits above.
  • Exposed timber beams and posts. Strip lights on the beams need a concealed feed: drilled down through the beam at the wall, or a shallow routed groove on the top face (CHECK with the builder before cutting a structural timber).
  • Cabinet wall. Devices R8 and S5 sit in the pantry's side panel; their cable has to drop from the joist line inside the pantry behind a cover.
  • Exterior walls are framed walls with sheathing and a membrane outside: drill studs in the middle, seal any penetration to outside lights and outlets.

Dwelling requirements that shape the circuits (NEC 2020 unless noted)

  • Receptacle spacing 210.52(A): no point along a wall more than 6' from a receptacle (so at most 12' apart), any wall 2' or wider counts.
  • Kitchen 210.52(B),(C): two or more 20 A small-appliance circuits; counter receptacles so no point is more than 2' from one (about 4' apart); all GFCI.
  • Bathroom 210.11(C)(3), 210.52(D): a 20 A circuit for bath receptacles; a receptacle within 3' of the basin; GFCI.
  • GFCI 210.8: bathrooms, kitchen counters, within 6' of a sink, outdoors.
  • AFCI 210.12: essentially every 120 V 15/20 A circuit in living areas, kitchens, bedrooms.
  • Neutral at every switch box 404.2(C): so no old-style two-wire switch loops; run the feed to the switch first, or 3-wire to it.
  • Three-way switching (the loft light, from the foot of the ladder and from beside the bed): 14-3 travellers between the two switch boxes. This is a single circuit, so it is fine on a 120 V-only panel.
  • Box fill 314.16: count conductors, devices, clamps and grounds; the three-gang box at the slider (S2) needs a deep box.
  • Outdoor boxes and covers weatherproof; in-use covers on outdoor receptacles.
  • Smoke and CO alarms: required in a dwelling (hardwired with battery backup in new work). Not yet on the plan. Wood stove = CO alarm.
  • Panel working space 110.26: 30" wide, 36" deep, 6'-6" high clear. Already kept in the layout.
