Notes on fit

  • Couch rug: 8' is 5" wider than the floor under the loft, so it runs that far past the loft edge toward the kitchen. A 7'-6" square would stay inside the loft edge. The front edge of an 8' rug ends at the foot of the ladder and about 3'-6" from the centre of the wood stove; check the stove's label for floor protection.
  • Couch rug and kitchen rug: the kitchen rug ends about 1" before the couch rug begins, and their corners line up for about 10".
  • Kitchen rug: leaves about 4" of floor each side in the aisle. It stops about 1'-8" short of the end of the counter and 2'-8" short of the first stool.
  • Loft rug: 7'-6" leaves about 1" in a 7'-7" loft, and the railing posts take about 1 1/2" of that at floor level. The rug has to slide under the railing's bottom rail and may need to curl slightly at the posts. Measure wall to railing post before ordering; rugs also run an inch or two off their stated size. If it is too tight, 6' x 9' is the next size down.
  • Loft rug: keep the front edge at least 1' back from where the ladder steps off, and use a rug pad.

Glass blocks (top of the cabinet wall)

  • One row between the loft joists, set at the bath face of the wall: 6 blocks, 8" x 8" nominal (7 3/4" actual), 3" to 4" thick, two per gap in three gaps.
  • Each gap is about 21" wide x 9 1/2" high, so wood trim makes up about 3" each side and under 1" top and bottom. Blocks about 6" wide (5 3/4" actual) would fit three per gap (9 blocks) with less trim.
  • Clear, wavy or frosted all work for privacy at that height (6'-8" and up). Acrylic blocks or a silicone-and-spacer kit are easier than mortar here.
  • Leave a soft joint (gasket or sealant) at the top: the loft floor above flexes.
  • The 8" deep ledge left on the living room side takes things up to about 9" tall and 7" deep.

Deck gates (front and back stairs)

Both gates are rolling gates that slide along the deck side of the railing, so the back one never crosses the red door's outward swing.

  • Hardware candidate: Pylex Sliding Gate Hardware Kit, model 11052 (Home Depot, $79.99 on 2026-10-06, black powder-coated steel, 5-year warranty): homedepot.com (one kit per gate, so two).
  • Sizing rule for this kit (from a buyer's review, not the listing): the gate must be 18" longer than the clear opening, and there must be room for that extra length beside the opening.
  • Gates to build (timber frame with balusters to match the railing):
    • Back: 48" clear opening -> 66" gate (5'-6"). Parks toward the porch side, where there is about 7' of railing.
    • Front: about 47" clear opening (one bay between evenly spaced posts, estimated from a photo: measure it) -> about 65" gate (5'-5"). Parks toward the porch side.
  • Materials per gate, scaled from the same review (their 54" gate took two 8' 2x4s and nine 30" balusters): the 66" gate needs about two 8' 2x4s and 11 balusters; the front gate about the same. Plus exterior screws.
  • Tips from that review: pre-drill with a 1/16" bit and go easy on the supplied screws (three snapped for them); it needs a square-drive bit that is not in the box; the gate can take a padlock.
  • NOT CONFIRMED (the listing's spec table is blank): maximum gate width and weight for one kit, though both gates are now close to the 54" example in the review.
