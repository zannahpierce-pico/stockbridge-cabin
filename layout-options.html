Stockbridge Cabin Layout Options

Second pass, after your notes and the photos of the cabin yours is based on. Fixed in every option: windows and doors, the wood stove, the loft floor, and the kitchen on the porch wall. The loft opening is not cut yet, so the way up is the thing that changes.

What changed from the first pass

  • No dining table. A couch, probably a narrow pull-out, with a low table in front of it. Two stools at a bar under the window are the place to eat.
  • The way up has to work for the dogs. A ship's ladder at 60° or more is hard for a dog going up and worse coming down. A straight stair at about 42° (9" rises, 10" treads) is walkable for most dogs. It needs a 7'-6" run and a slot in the loft floor about 2' by 7'.
  • The bath has room for a 32" shower as well as a toilet and sink. Options B and C use the designer's add-a-bath plan, 6'-10" by 5'-0" across the back wall. Its wall lands right beside the red door, which is why that door is where it is.

Shared by all three

  • Panel on the middle of the porch wall, with its clear floor in front. The battery is on wheels and sits on the floor under the panel; it rolls out of the way when the panel needs work.
  • Kitchen: a tall pantry on the back wall beside the red door, about 6' of counter under the two windows with the sink and cooktop, an under-counter fridge, then the counter carries on along the wall as a bar with two stools facing the window. Nothing sticks out into the room.
  • One chair by the slider, outside the stove's 3' zone (the dashed circle).

Option A: your plan, with a real stair Closest to what you drew.

What it is

  • A storage stair where your ladder and boxes were, along the loft edge, climbing toward the back wall.
  • The couch against the bath wall and a short seat under the low window, as you had them.
  • The designer's bath does not fit beside this stair, so the bath is narrower and deeper: 4'-11" by 5'-6", with the shower at the front and the door at the end of a hall behind the top of the stair.

Gains

  • Drawers and cupboards under the whole stair.
  • The stair screens the couch from the kitchen, like the bench behind the couch in the reference cabin.

Costs

  • The stair foot leaves about 2'-5" beside the end of the peninsula, which is the main route through the cabin.
  • The room under the loft is 5'-3" wide beside the stair, so the couch looks down a corridor toward the stove.
  • The bath is a custom layout, and you reach it by walking behind the stair.

Option B: stair on the bank wall My pick.

What it is

  • The same stair, moved against the bank wall under the loft. Its foot is beside the low window.
  • The couch floats under the loft edge facing the stove and the front glass, as in the reference cabin.
  • The designer's bath, same size and door, with the fixtures mirrored to clear the window.

Gains

  • The kitchen aisle and the peninsula are untouched.
  • The couch gets the fire and the view.
  • The space behind the couch, about 4'-6" square, is free for dog beds, coats and gear.

Costs

  • The loft opening is beside the two loft windows, and the bed moves to the open side of the loft, so that edge needs a solid guard.
  • The lowest steps cross the bottom of the low window.
  • The couch has no wall behind it.

Option C: hinged ladder by the stove The one being built in 3D.

What it is

  • A plain straight ladder leaning on the bank wall, just in front of the loft's edge, as in the reference cabin. You climb facing the wall and step sideways onto the loft at its very corner. It hinges at the top, so it can swing flat against the wall.
  • Nothing is cut out of the loft.
  • The designer's bath made 6" deeper and 4" narrower (so its side wall clears the back door), 6'-6" by 5'-0", with its door in the side wall facing the entry. The shower is at the entry end, and a 24" sink and the toilet are side by side on the bank wall. Its front wall is a 12"-deep cabinet wall built now: a full-height pantry at the kitchen end, bookshelves open to the living room above 4'-7", and cubbies open to the bath below. The back-wall window is left clear.
  • Your fold-out couch (78.8" wide, 39.4" deep) sits against the bath wall and faces the stove and the front glass. The dashed outline shows it opened as a bed. A low cushion chair sits under the large front window beside the hearth.

Gains

  • The loft keeps its whole floor.
  • The floor under the loft in front of the bath is one open room, about 7'-7" by 8'-0".
  • With the ladder stowed, the front of the room is clear.

Costs

  • The dogs cannot use it.
  • Its foot stands about 1'-7" out from the wall, beside the hearth and about 8" from the corner of the stove as the stove sits in the model. Check that against the stove's label and where the stove really is.

The loft in each option Heavy lines are railings. Dashed lines show where the sloped ceiling is 5' and 6' above the loft floor.

Option A

  • The loft is 7'-7" wide. A 2' stair slot leaves 5'-3", so a queen fits against the bank wall with about 3" to spare. You get in from the foot of the bed.
  • The stair comes up under the low side of the roof. Headroom over the top steps is about 5', so you arrive stooping.
  • The slot is about 6'-10" long, because the stair needs open space above it for most of its run.
  • Left over: a 5'-3" by 4'-6" patch of floor between the landing and the bed, and a 2'-4" strip beside the bed at the front.

Option B

  • The slot is on the bank wall, under the high side of the roof, so you come up with about 6' to 6'-6" over your head.
  • The queen sits on the low side, where you are lying down anyway, with its side against the stair guard.
  • The back 5' of the loft stays one open piece of floor, 7'-7" wide.
  • The bed is at the open edge of the loft, against the railing.

Option C

  • No slot, so the loft is a clear 7'-7" by 13'-9".
  • The ladder leans on the bank wall in front of the loft's edge and runs 2' above the loft floor as a handhold. You step off sideways at the corner, where the roof is highest: about 6'-4" overhead.
  • A queen (5'-0" by 6'-8") goes at the back with its head on the back wall, so you look out the front windows from bed. It is centred across the loft, with about 1'-3" on each side: enough to sidle in, not to walk. The front keeps 6'-10" of open floor.
  • Railings run along both open edges of the loft, with a gap where the ladder steps off. A shelf on the back wall on each side of the bed holds the bedside lights.

Things I assumed

  • The stair is 2' wide with 9" rises. That is steeper than a house stair. Small or older dogs do better with 7" to 8" rises, which adds about 2' of run and does not fit option A without moving the peninsula.
  • The designer's bath is drawn mirrored: shower at the entry end, toilet under the back-wall window, sink in the bank corner. His drawing has the shower in the corner, but your window sits there with its sill about 1'-8" up, and a low window inside a shower stall is a problem. The window position is from a photo, so tape it before framing.
  • The stove needs about 3' to anything that burns. Its label may allow less.
  • The couch is 5' to 6' long and 3' deep. Your narrow pull-out may be smaller, which helps every option.
  • The fridge is under the counter. A full-height one would take the middle of the porch wall, and the panel would move to the back wall beside the red door.
