Stockbridge Cabin Cabinet Wall

Shop drawing, cut list and materials for the built-in that divides the bath from the living room: a pantry at the kitchen end, bookshelves and the back of the couch on the living room side, clothes cubbies on the bath side, and a row of glass blocks between the loft joists above. Built now, before the bath, as the bath's front wall.

How it is put together

One plywood box, 6'-9 7/8" long, 12" deep and 6'-8 1/8" high, standing on a hollow plinth and stopping 1/4" under the loft joists. Its two faces do different jobs, and the split board at 54 7/8" off the floor is where they swap: below it the living room side is a closed panel (the back of the couch) and the bath side is open cubbies; above it the bath side is closed and the living room side is open bookshelves. The pantry at the kitchen end is closed on both long faces and open only toward the kitchen.

  1. Plinth. Two rails and seven blocks, 3 5/8" to the top of the bottom panel. Leave it hollow: the conduit from the bank wall runs through it to the pantry (electrical plan). Level it with shims and screw it to the floor.
  2. Carcass. Bottom on the plinth. Stand the end panel at the bank wall, the pantry back, and the two pantry sides; screw the top down over everything. The box is now 6'-9 7/8" long and the bay opening between the pantry back and the end panel is where the three bays go.
  3. Lower half. The two lower dividers stand on the bottom, 11 1/4" deep and flush with the bath face, splitting the opening into three equal bays of 20 9/16". Cut the six cubby shelves to fit each bay and pocket-screw them from underneath. Then the couch-back panel closes the living room side: a full 48" sheet width standing on a 3 1/4" filler strip, so the only seam is 6 7/8" off the floor, hidden behind the couch.
  4. Split board. Full depth, continuous, resting on the lower dividers and the couch-back panel. Both its long edges show, so band them.
  5. Upper half. The two upper dividers stand on the split board directly over the lower ones, flush with the living room face. Three middle shelves cut to fit. The closing panel on the bath side goes on last, from the split board up to the underside of the top; the towel bars screw into it.
  6. Pantry shelves. Four, on pocket screws or shelf pins; the top one is only 5 7/8" clear, meant for the LED driver and flat things. The pantry's living-room side panel carries the dimmer, the outlet and the plug-in sconce: cut those openings before assembly.
  7. Glass-block row. After the cabinet is in and screwed to the bank wall: nail a plywood cheek to each side of each joist bay, set 12" deep less 3/4" from the bath face. Build each frame to the measured bay (about 20 1/2" between the cheeks: two blocks, a 2 3/8" filler each side, a 15/16" strip top and bottom), set the blocks in silicone with spacers, and leave a soft gasket joint under the loft boards, which flex. The 8 1/2" left in front of the frame is the ledge on the living room side.
  8. Fixing. Three screws through the end panel into the bank-wall studs, two up through each cheek into its joist, and the plinth to the floor. Nothing is fixed through the bath face: the future bath side wall just lands on the pantry's bath-side panel, 3 5/8" from the kitchen end.

Cut list every panel 3/4" birch plywood unless noted; length runs with the face grain

Shelves and dividers are listed at their drawn size, but cut the shelves after the dividers are in and measure each bay: a plywood "3/4" is usually 23/32", and the bays only stay equal if the dividers are placed from the drawing, not from the shelves.

Check on site before cutting

  • Height to the joists. The drawing has the joist undersides at 80 3/8" and the loft boards at 89 3/4", off the model. Measure at both ends of the wall line and at the middle; the cabinet height follows the lowest point, less 1/4".
  • Joist spacing. The three bays are drawn 22" wide with 4" joists. Each block frame is built to its own bay, so only the cheeks are cut ahead.
  • The bank wall. Check it is plumb across the 12" depth; scribe the end panel to it rather than shimming the whole cabinet off it.
  • The floor. The plinth takes up the slope. Mark the cabinet's line on the floor first: the bath face is 4'-11 5/8" from the back wall, moved 2" forward from the designer's line so the bath door can be a stock prehung 24".
  • Baskets. The cubbies are 20 9/16" x 11 1/4" deep; the shopping list's 15"-high x 11"-deep baskets still fit, but the measured bays decide the width.
  • Electrical first. The holes in the pantry back and the plinth rail, the device openings in the pantry's side panels and the tape channel under the top and the middle shelf are all easier before assembly: see the electrical plan for positions.
Drawn, not builtEvery number here comes from the 3D model, tidied to 1/8" and to 3/4" plywood. The model's own dividers are 3/8" out of line between the two halves and its bays differ by 3/4"; this drawing stacks the dividers and makes the bays equal, which changes nothing anyone will notice. The glass-block frames assume 3 1/8" acrylic blocks; thicker glass blocks need a deeper frame and a narrower ledge.
